Case study: L3SA

L3SA is a woman- and veteran-owned rope access training center in Spring, Texas, running SPRAT and IRATA certification classes. Their old site was a single Google Sites page: an about paragraph, a phone number, and a note to email for class dates. Every sign-up meant back and forth by phone and email.

What changed

  • Before: "email for dates"A live class scheduleUpcoming classes with open seats, prices and dates. When a class fills, students can join a standby list.
  • Before: phone tagOnline sign-upStudents sign up from their phone. They get an invoice by email and are registered once the deposit is paid.
  • Before: noneCrew sign-upsA manager can book a whole crew at once, with one invoice for the group.
  • Before: one lineA full Spanish versionEvery page and every email in English and Spanish, one tap to switch. Many of their students prefer Spanish.
  • Before: noneAn admin page for the ownersSee sign-ups, deposits and seats, and manage classes without touching code or a site builder.

The rebuild is in final review with the owners and launches at l3sa.com. Their domain and Google email stay theirs, and email kept working through the move.

Why not a template builder?

Square Online, Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy are good tools for getting a business online quickly. The trouble starts when the business needs something their templates and add-ons don't do. A custom build doesn't have that ceiling: it is made to the exact request.

Template builder

  • Your site fits their layouts
  • Features come from their add-on store, if one exists
  • A monthly plan, with add-ons on top
  • You make the updates yourself
  • Moving away usually means starting over

Custom rebuild

  • The site is designed around how your business works
  • If the feature doesn't exist, it gets built
  • You keep your domain, your email and your content
  • Changes are one message away
  • You deal with the person who writes the code

How it works

It goes the same way whether you're replacing a site or starting with nothing. With no site yet, the build starts from the ground up: a domain, a design shaped around the business, and the features you need from day one.

  1. Talk it throughYou explain what the site needs to do. If you have a site, I go through it first to see what's working and what isn't.
  2. Domain and planHave a domain? It stays yours, right where it is. Need one? I help you pick a name and get it registered for the business.
  3. Private previewThe site takes shape on a private link you click through on your own phone. You send feedback before anything goes live.
  4. Go liveThe new site goes up on your domain. If you're moving off an old site, email keeps working through the switch.
  5. Keep it currentChanges, fixes and new features on request, for as long as you need them.
